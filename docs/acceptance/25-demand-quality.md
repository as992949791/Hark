# Demand quality and real research-path acceptance

2026-10-18, [issue 22](https://github.com/as992949791/Hark/issues/22), baseline `v0.1.35-hark-identity`. This release filters source context, combines repeated needs with supporting quotations, qualifies concrete product requirements, and validates pain-theme and real interface behavior. Raw posts, provider responses and screenshots remain private/ignored.

## Implemented behavior

- A contextual review replaces the previous final grouping call. Source purpose precedes fragment sentiment: tutorials, recommendations given to others, seller promotion and unclear voices cannot support published demand. Positive history alone is not a complaint. Mixed recommendation posts require particular care; model semantics are still fallible.
- Repeated expressions of one concrete need from one observed author become one case, preserving every accepted quotation. The server also merges matching author/problem labels. Distinct needs/authors and unrelated unknown-author documents stay separate; this is not proof of independently verified human identities.
- Product fit enumerates exact quoted requirements. Supported/contradicted requirements need an exact saved capability/pain fact; audience overlap is insufficient. Missing price/platform/feature support remains unknown. Explicitly replacing the supplied product prevents a positive fit. Topic requests use a dedicated schema with empty requirements and null product fields.
- Case/problem links generate themes; recurring requires at least two independent observed identities. Duplicate quotations and unknown authors cannot inflate that count. Old results remain readable with an unreviewed warning and without legacy fit certifications.
- An explicit saved-original option clones only an owned, nonexpired terminal run's demand documents, not prior assessments or charges. Original collection and expiry times remain unchanged. It does not repeat searches or fill inaccessible pages. New URLs may still require one preparation page/profile.
